Numerical and statistical validation

Validation asks three different questions: whether a change preserves a specified calculation, whether the calculation satisfies its physical and accounting contracts, and whether complete showers have compatible distributions. Each requires its own reference and acceptance rule. Agreement with a frozen implementation establishes a regression result; it does not independently establish the accuracy of the underlying model.

The implemented checks below refer to source revision d5297c43c041a22b43c65c322d9b51bc7ec8e397. They describe test assertions, not results from a new test run. The complete-shower protocol at the end is a recommended procedure, separate from those assertions.

Align the physical problem

Before comparing backends, retain the resolved configuration and match the following quantities. Configure a simulation describes the application options; c8_air_shower.cpp supplies the air application’s construction and process order.

Configuration

Required agreement

Primary

Particle or nuclear species, total energy or energy-distribution law, injection position, direction, time and forced-interaction/decay policy. The air application’s primary energy is total energy in GeV; for a nucleus it is the whole nucleus’s energy.

Transport and cuts

Kinetic tracking cuts for EM particles, hadrons, muons and taus; neutrino tracking; time cuts; magnetic deflection and continuous-step policies. The air application sets the PROPOSAL production threshold to the minimum of its four tracking-cut options. Equal primary energy does not compensate for unequal production thresholds.

Stopped positrons

The native/PROPOSAL transport policy has EnableStoppedPositronAnnihilation = false in PositronAtRest.hpp. This controls extra photons from positrons removed at the cut; in-flight annihilation remains a separate process. Do not assume an independently configured EM model has the same termination policy.

Models and tables

EM model, hadronic and decay models, high-/low-energy model transition, model-data versions, material composition and density, production thresholds, native/auxiliary table identities and interpolation settings. Changing from PROPOSAL to EGS4 is a model comparison as well as an execution comparison. See Physics models and table preparation.

Thinning

Threshold, maximum weight and zero-weight-particle policy. In the air application, --emthin 0 disables thinning; --max-weight 0 selects an automatic weight limit. The default thinning fraction is nonzero. Weighted particle sums and their fluctuations must be interpreted using the actual thinning configuration.

Environment and observation

Atmosphere or material profile, interfaces, magnetic field, coordinate system, injection geometry, observation surface and escape boundaries. A material interface and an absorbing observation plane have different meanings. Match longitudinal depth bins and --profile-crossings (both, forward or original-c8).

Radio

Emission formalism, propagation model and table fingerprint, observer positions, component basis, time origin, sample rate, duration, frequency grid where applicable, refinement and accumulation policy. Match reflection/branch choices for firn. An empty observer list or a truncated time window cannot validate a radio signal.

Keep execution choices alongside this physics record: backend, compiler, floating-point options, device architecture, thread count, batching, CPU fallback policy, worker partitioning and lifetime. Some choices can change rounding, scheduling or random-stream progression without changing the intended physics.

Implemented unit and module checks

The suite uses several kinds of reference. Read the assertion and fixture together before interpreting a passing target.

Check

Scope of the assertion

testStochasticContracts.cpp

Philox golden words, production process/draw identities, every bit of the key fields, reversed device traversal, host/device random results and open-interval uniforms. This tests the implemented random mapping and routing contracts, not shower-distribution agreement.

ContinuousChecks.hpp and continuous query tests

Current and frozen production queries run on the same execution space. Floating results use exact equality, with signed-zero checks and matching NaN classification; state fields also match exactly. Native-model diagnostic errors are reported separately and explicitly identified as not migration tolerances. Real-table query targets require CORSIKA_ENABLE_PROPOSAL_EXPORT; see continuous/CMakeLists.txt.

testPhotonCascade.cpp and testLeptonCascade.cpp

Queue ownership, pending inputs, checkpoints, histories and multi-front execution against reference drivers. Native-table cases are conditional on PROPOSAL export. These are component cascades and scheduling regressions, not an ensemble of complete application showers.

testProfileAccumulation.cpp and testLeptonStatistics.cpp

Profile and ledger accumulation, known totals, fixed-point overflow, counters, and fused versus separate accumulation. The material-boundary fixture in testMaterialBoundaryStatistics.cpp uses real firn geometry with an exact linear range fixture to isolate record accounting from cross-section accuracy.

testRadioProjection.cpp and testRadioAccumulator.cpp

Projection against the frozen implementation and native C8 CoREAS/ZHS modules; deterministic and floating accumulation, capacity and rejection contracts. testFullWaveAccumulator.cpp separately compares firn full-wave spectra with a CPU response calculation on a supplied or generated response-table fixture.

Some concrete tolerances illustrate why one global numerical threshold is inappropriate:

  • The firn material-boundary fixture requires distance error below \(10^{-12}\) m and grammage error below \(10^{-10}\) g/cm². Its fused and separate fixed-point histogram and counter results are equal.

  • The photon muon-pair materialization case in testPhotonFinalState.cpp checks the weighted child energy after subtracting the recorded mass convention correction against the weighted parent energy, with a bound of \(8\epsilon\) times that parent energy. Here \(\epsilon\) is std::numeric_limits<double>::epsilon().

  • The native radio projection comparison bounds the maximum sample error in each observer/component by floor + 2e-8 * reference_peak. The floor is \(2\times10^{-18}\) for CoREAS electric field and \(2\times10^{-27}\) for ZHS vector potential, in their respective SI units. The separate comparison against frozen projection code is exact.

  • The floating radio accumulator comparison uses 1e-28 + 2e-12 * reference_peak per component. Deterministic waveform comparison and exported fixed-point arrays use equality. This statement concerns the test’s current/reference pair on the same execution space; it is not a cross-device bitwise guarantee.

  • The firn full-wave fixture requires relative \(L_2\) error below \(2\times10^{-10}\) for total, direct and boundary complex spectra. Its definition is \(\sqrt{\sum|C-R|^2/\max(\sum|R|^2,10^{-300})}\) over observers, components and frequencies. It also checks reset/replay and batch-size equivalence for that fixture.

These bounds belong to the named tests and quantities. They are neither universal physics-error estimates nor acceptance thresholds for arbitrary complete-shower outputs. Fixed-point addition removes summation-order dependence for identical quantized contributions within range; it cannot make different trajectories or differently rounded contributions identical.

Random streams and reproducibility

A seed is only one part of a reproducibility record. The Kokkos EM RandomNumberKey contains seed, shower ID, particle history ID, step ID, process ID and draw ID. Philox.hpp and Philox.inl map this identity to random words without using the particle’s queue position. Changing a process decision, particle genealogy or draw identity can still change subsequent histories.

Execution mode

Stream policy to record

Direct application, -N > 1

Native CPU streams are seeded once before the event loop and continue across events. The direct loop supplies successive shower IDs to execution. It does not reseed the native RNG with seed + event_index.

Coordinator events

The coordinator uses first_seed + event_index for successive event seeds. worker_seed(seed, index) hashes the decimal seed:index string with SHA-256, takes the first eight bytes little-endian, masks to 63 bits and replaces zero by one. GPU worker indices start at zero; parallel CPU-prefix worker indices use 1024 + i. See AirShowerCoordinator.cpp and StaticFrontierIO.cpp.

Persistent GPU workers

Each request sets the seed for device sampling and selected CPU fallback replay. Native CPU streams, copied model callbacks and SOPHIA’s private stream continue from worker initialization. They are not rewound to the request seed. WorkerSession.inl records continue-worker-streams and the CPU initial seed.

Consequently, event two of a direct multi-event process, a fresh process with an incremented seed, and a request in a persistent worker are different stream histories. Record the event order, worker IDs, partitions, skips and reuse policy when reproducing a campaign. WorkerSeedTest.cpp checks hash vectors, CLI/JSON preservation and a finite collision-regression sample; WorkerSessionTest.cpp checks native and copied-stream continuation. A finite seed scan does not establish that collisions are impossible.

For deterministic debugging, fix the input particles or track spool, physical configuration, keys and stream state, then compare decisions, records and accumulators at the first divergence. testDecisionReplayTape.cpp tests decision-tape serialization and preserved wire data. A successful tape round-trip establishes that diagnostic contract; it is not an assertion that every backend or scheduler replays a whole shower identically.

Use statistical acceptance when histories or stream policies differ. Identical top-level seed numbers alone do not justify treating outputs as event-by-event numerical replicas. See Process coordination and worker lifecycle for worker lifecycle and Track collection and observer preparation for radio-track replay.

Energy accounting and numerical residuals

Distinguish a comparison residual from an energy-closure residual. For a candidate value \(C_i\) and reference \(R_i\), define the signed comparison residual as \(d_i=C_i-R_i\). Report absolute errors in physical units; for a normalized error, state the denominator and its behavior at zero. For example,

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} e_i &= \frac{|C_i-R_i|}{\max(|R_i|,q_0)},\\[0.6em] e_2 &= \frac{\left(\sum_i|C_i-R_i|^2\right)^{1/2}} {\left(\sum_i|R_i|^2\right)^{1/2}}. \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

The chosen \(q_0>0\) has the observable’s units and must be documented. The second expression is defined only for a nonzero reference norm; report an absolute norm for a zero reference. Neither expression supplies an acceptance threshold by itself. Require finite values before computing these metrics, unless the particular contract explicitly expects a nonfinite value.

The air application’s legacy log reports \(100[(E_{\mathrm{deposit}}+E_{\mathrm{ground}})/E_0-1]\). This is a signed percentage difference, not the strict EM ledger below. It omits explicit terms needed by that ledger and cannot be interpreted as a universal conservation test.

ShowerReport.inl defines the resident EM ledger directly:

1242    auto const ledgerSourceGeV = ledgerInitialGeV + routerStats.mediumRestMassInputGeV_ +
1243                                 routerStats.massConventionCorrectionGeV_;
1244    auto const ledgerTerminalGeV =

1247        routerStats.unwrittenPhotoelectricBindingEnergyGeV_ -
1248        routerStats.observationCutOverlapEnergyGeV_;
1249    auto const ledgerResidualGeV = ledgerSourceGeV - ledgerTerminalGeV;
1250    auto const ledgerRelativeError =
1251        std::abs(ledgerResidualGeV) /
1252        std::max(ledgerSourceGeV, std::numeric_limits<double>::min());
1253    auto const ledgerCompleteCoverage =
1254        is_em(beamCode) && emthinfrac <= 0. && scalarEmSteps == 0 &&
1255        routerStats.particlesReturnedForCpuFallback_ == 0 &&
1256        routerStats.particlesReturnedForCpuMemorySpill_ == 0 &&
1257        routerStats.specifiedCpuFinalStates_ == 0;

The signed residual is source minus terminal, in GeV. The normalized closure error takes its absolute value; the denominator’s floor is the smallest positive normal double, not machine epsilon. Medium electron rest mass is a physical input for the accounted atomic-electron processes. The signed mass-convention correction records conversion between PROPOSAL total energies and the C8 kinetic-energy stack. Cut rest mass and otherwise unwritten photoelectric binding energy are explicit sinks; observation/cut overlap is subtracted to avoid double counting. ProfileResult.hpp documents these terms. A worker’s initial energy is the weighted sum of its imported roots, rather than the global primary energy.

The implemented energy_ledger.acceptance_tolerance is \(10^{-4}\). accepted additionally requires complete_coverage: an EM primary, no thinning, no scalar EM steps, no returned CPU fallback or memory spill, and no specified CPU final states. The report throws when complete coverage holds and the relative error exceeds this tolerance. If coverage is false, accepted: false does not by itself diagnose failed energy conservation; the reported resident ledger is insufficient for that whole shower. Do not apply this gate to a hadronic or thinned shower without a ledger covering its remaining processes and weighting conventions.

Recommended complete-shower protocol

The following procedure is proposed for accepting a physics or backend change. It is not an implemented universal ensemble gate at this revision. In particular, the coordinator’s compare.py exercises closed synthetic inputs and process/output handling, and explicitly makes no shower claim. COMPLETE.json and successful unit tests are prerequisites for a campaign, not its statistical conclusion.

  1. Define the claim and reference. Specify whether the comparison changes execution, the physics model or both. Freeze the configuration above and choose representative primary species, energies, directions and material conditions for the claimed scope. Include boundary and observation cases when those paths change. Establish the intended observables, uncertainty method, sample size and scientifically meaningful equivalence bounds before inspecting candidate results.

  2. Check complete events. Retain commands, resolved seeds and stream policy, software/table hashes, completion records, logs and raw outputs. Inspect pending queues, fallback/spill counts, invalid records and accumulation overflows. Identify expected CPU-completed processes rather than requiring every fallback counter to be zero. Report failed or excluded events and their reason; do not silently compare only successful survivors.

  3. Compare physical observables. Use energy-ledger terms where coverage permits; longitudinal particle and deposited-energy profiles; observation particle multiplicities, energy and spatial distributions; and radio waveforms or spectra. Include distributions of derived quantities, such as the depth of a profile maximum, when relevant. Matching one integrated total does not constrain profile shape, tails or waveform timing.

  4. Quantify sampling uncertainty. Use showers as the sampling units. For independent reference/candidate samples, the estimated standard error of a mean difference is \(\sqrt{s_C^2/n_C+s_R^2/n_R}\). For a justified paired design, estimate the uncertainty from the within-pair differences instead. State how pairs were constructed; a shared seed label is insufficient. Preserve bin and antenna correlations when resampling whole showers or using covariance matrices. Thinned weights do not make profile sums independent Poisson counts. Check the assumptions of the chosen uncertainty method.

  5. Apply the declared acceptance rule. Report differences with confidence intervals, distributions and effect sizes, including tails where needed. To claim equivalence, require the uncertainty interval to fit within the predeclared equivalence bounds. A test that does not reject a difference is not evidence that an arbitrarily small difference has been excluded. Account for multiple bins, observers and observables in the test design; distinguish exploratory diagnostics from the acceptance criteria.

Profile comparisons must use the same depth grid and crossing convention. Deposited energy in energyloss/dEdX.parquet is GeV per bin; division by bin width is required for a density per unit slant depth. Retain particle weights and the distinction between production counts, transported-particle crossings and observation records.

Radio comparisons must retain signed components on compatible time grids. The standard accumulator returns CoREAS electric field in V/m and ZHS vector potential in V s/m; compare fields only after the defined ZHS differentiation and endpoint convention. State any filtering, interpolation or time shift, and report timing offsets before removing them. Compare per-observer peak, norm and waveform residuals as appropriate; a small aggregate norm can hide a discrepancy at a weak observer. Combine coherent fields before computing squared-field quantities such as fluence. Equal fluence alone does not establish equal waveform phase, polarization or arrival time. See Parallel projection and waveform accumulation and Propagation models and medium-specific interfaces.
